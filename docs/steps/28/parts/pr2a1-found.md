# Step 28, its parts: extensions found and pinned

The first part of step 28's second pull request ([answered.md](../answered.md), "The design, before building", point 10, "Extensions in a session"): the extensions a session finds itself, each checked against its pin, what each registered taken from the registration cache, the project's extensions in the trust question, `cineris.state`, and a change seen at the next turn. Skills and sections 6 and 7 of the system prompt are planned for the part after it, and hooks for the one after that.

## What was built

- **The user's own pins** (`cineris-policy`'s `trust::PinStore`).
  `pins.toml` in the state directory, `version = 1`, one `[[pin]]` of kind, name, hash and time each, written owner-only and atomically under `pins.toml.lock` and read strictly, a kind this build does not know kept and written back.
  It is a file of its own, so that a build that reads `trust.toml` up to version 2 is not made to refuse it ([06-permissions-and-sandbox.md](../../../architecture/06-permissions-and-sandbox.md), "Project trust").
  `TrustStore::pinned_hashes` gives every trusted project's pins of a kind, for pruning the cache.
  Beside it: `cineris_ext::source::Source`, the user's or the project's, which keys an extension's pin and its values; and `[extensions.<name>] enabled`, the user's alone, taking effect at the next session.
- **Discovery and the registration cache** (`cineris-ext`'s `discover` and `cache`).
  Each folder directly under the user's `<config dir>/extensions/` and the project's `.cineris/extensions/` read whole, its manifest giving the folder's own name; roots judged where they lead, a project's inside the workspace; no link followed into an extension's folder; a root that is not there ruled out with a look or two, before the path resolver is asked; names that differ only in case refused within one source.
  The cache, `<state dir>/extensions/registered.json`, by the hash of each folder, read strictly and set aside whole when it cannot be read in full, written owner-only and atomically, pruned of the hashes no pin names ([12-extensions.md](../../../architecture/12-extensions.md), "Tier 4: script extensions").
- **`cineris.state`** (the core's `ext::state`).
  `session` values as the log's `ext` records, which a rewind leaves and a fork copies as they were at its point (`cineris_session::Log::fork`); `project` values in `projects/<project key>/extensions/<source>-<name>.json`, read strictly; 64 KiB a scope, a key of 1 to 256 bytes, a hundred sets a call and 16 MiB of `session` records a session ([decision 0041](../../../decisions/0041-an-extension-s-state-is-kept-in-the-session-s-records-and-a-project-file-read-strictly-within-fixed-bounds.md)).
  `CoreOptions::ext_session_budget` is a test's, which only tightens the 16 MiB.
- **The core's pipeline and the trust question** (`ext::found`, `assemble::look`).
  A task each session starts once its loop runs: the folders found, every standing decided against `pins.toml` and `trust.toml`, approved extensions' tools taken from the cache, one the cache misses evaluated once and the first turn held for it, the cache written and pruned after.
  The trust question lists a project's extensions in words from their manifests and pins them as it read them; `cineris permissions trust` shows them as the question does and pins them on a yes at a terminal, or with `--with-extensions` where there is none.
  The interactive application's dialog names a changed extension apart from changed rules, and says what a keep of one does; the protocol's doc comments say the same.
- **Hot-reload** (`ext::watched`).
  What the extensions were found in, stamped without following a link and looked at in order at each turn's start; a change has every folder found and judged again, an extension that no longer stands approved withdrawn and one that now does loaded before the turn ([12-extensions.md](../../../architecture/12-extensions.md), "Hot-reload").
- **The breaks:** the parts' lists (`step-28-pins`, `step-28-found`, `step-28-state`, `step-28-pinned`) are put together in the step's, [mutations/step-28.breaks](../../../../mutations/step-28.breaks).

**Settled by the maintainers during the build:**

1. `cineris permissions trust` pins a project's extensions.
   It prints each with what it may do, in the question's words, and pins them only on a yes at a terminal, no being the default; at a terminal `--with-extensions` is ignored, so that nobody scripting at a terminal expects the flag to answer.
   Where there is no terminal to ask at, it pins them only with `--with-extensions`, and otherwise trusts the rules and says the extensions were left unapproved.
2. *Keep trusting it, without what is new* holds for an extension for the process alone, with no field of `trust.toml`: the next start asks again.
3. `--trust-project` approves no extension that is not pinned, and the session's notice names `cineris permissions trust`.
4. A yes the trust store could not keep approves the extensions the question listed, by the hashes shown, for that session alone (`ForTheRun::extensions`), the early review's recommendation.
5. The finding's task ends with its session and with the core; a session closed while its registration waits neither blocks nor leaks into the next session of the core (`extensions_trust::a_session_closed_while_its_registration_waits_neither_blocks_nor_leaks`).
6. Within one source, extensions whose names differ only in case are all refused, on every system (a Windows test on a case-sensitive folder, on a runner).
7. `cineris.state`'s bounds and where its values live: [decision 0041](../../../decisions/0041-an-extension-s-state-is-kept-in-the-session-s-records-and-a-project-file-read-strictly-within-fixed-bounds.md).
8. Extensions are found off the path that opens the session, and the start-up tests count what it reads, with no extension and with some (below).
9. After the full review: none is read once 16 MiB have been read from a root; the trust question keeps no bytes of a folder but its prompt file; and the session reads a project's folder again only to register it, at the approved hash ("The full review", below).
10. After the full review: the words of an approval where an extension's programs are not confined, and for a `*` and a `*.domain` in `net`; and one helper for every path and name shown as code, which `cineris ext add` is to use too.

## What is left open, and where

- **For the hooks part:** the wait for a session's extensions has no failed state; a changed extension's pinned registration is not kept, which its gating hooks need in order to fail closed ([answered.md](../answered.md), point 5); misses are registered one after another, each given up to the 10 s an evaluation is; a hook's host calls must reach `cineris.state` through the same store as a tool's, with the extension's source; in a hook, a host call the engine would ask about becomes a refusal.
- **`cineris ext add` is the third pull request's.** Until then nothing in the harness writes `pins.toml`, and the notice that a user's extension is not approved names that command.
- **A folder swapped for a link and back while `Snapshot::read` reads it** is followed, a narrower race since the full review, which looks at the folder again after the read and leaves out one that is a link then. It is benign here, since what is read is used only if its hash equals a pin; the third pull request's `cineris ext add`, which pins the snapshot it shows, reads the folder through handles that follow no link.
- **For the next part (skills, and sections 6 and 7 of the system prompt):** section 7 is an extension's prompt text, which a session takes from the registration cache. It takes it only from an entry that fits the extension as it was read (`Entry::fits`, the prompt file's text among it), and it is the author's text: the next part shows it labelled as the extension's, with what acts unseen escaped, as the trust question shows the description, and never as the harness's words.
- **The look before a runtime starts is a look, not a hold.** `Snapshot::read` still follows a link on the way to the extension's folder, so a folder swapped between the look and the read is read through; what is read is checked against the approved hash, so nothing unapproved runs. The stamps' walk of a folder (`read_dir`) has the same window. The third pull request's read through handles that follow no link closes both.
- **A trusted project's `sandbox.write` can turn the sandbox off for its sessions.** A write root that holds the harness's files makes a session not enforce at all ([decision 0032](../../../decisions/0032-what-the-linux-sandbox-needs.md)), and a project's write roots apply once it is trusted: its shell commands and its extensions' programs then run unconfined, and `autonomous` is refused. The trust question now says so of an extension's programs, and, settled by the maintainers after the fresh review, warns of such a root before the yes: "This project's configuration asks to write in `~/.cargo`, which holds Cineris's own files. If you trust it, its sessions run without the sandbox.", first among what it lists, a resource of kind `settings`, and `cineris permissions trust` prints the same (`extensions_review::the_trust_question_judges_the_sandbox_by_the_load_a_yes_puts_in_force`; `pinned-sandbox-off-unwarned`). Leaving such a root out and going on enforcing amends decision 0032 and step 26's tests, and is the maintainers' item for step 32 (the item `a-projects-write-root-holding-the-harness-files-turns-the-sandbox-off` (settled in [the third security fix](../../security-fix-3/answered.md))).
- **A write root that cannot be placed** (`~` where there is no home folder, `~otheruser`) counts as holding the harness's files, at a session's start and in the trust question's warning alike: what cannot be judged is not granted, and the warning then names the root as it is written.
- **The question's names can be cut where the session's are not:** `discover::names_seeing` bounds the user's folder by places alone, and the session's `look_in` stops reading past 16 MiB of it first, so a user's folder past 16 MiB and 4,096 places is cut for the question and not for the session. It fails closed (the question takes every name, and lists no project's extension), but the session's notice of that extension then points at a question that will not list it.
- **What the full review's fixes left without a test of their own:** the questions about a script's write and fetch and the core's notices of a working folder outside the workspace, a workspace refused and a shell passed over now show their paths through `in_code`, with no test of a backtick in one; and the session's one turn's start at a time has no test that two clients' prompts race.
- **Lower points:** the folders discovery left out are said again in every session; the command a notice names is not quoted for a folder whose name has a space; no test has two writers change one project's file of `cineris.state` at once, nor checks that a `CoreOptions::ext_session_budget` over 16 MiB is held to it; a configuration directory on a share the path resolver does not allow leaves the user's folder out, with the resolver's reason.

## The tests folded for Windows' tier 2

A `try` run of both trees on one Windows runner (44873812174) measured the part's integration tests at 5.1 s of the tier's 40 s, a test about 60 ms. The extension crate's discovery and cache tests are its unit tests now (`discover::tests`, `cache::on_disk`, tier 1), two tests of the trust question's words are unit tests of `ext::found::in_words`, the extensions' sessions are kept in memory, and tests that shared a set-up are one world each, their cases its steps (the user's extension's standing, the cache's taking and pruning, the never and the not now, the configuration directory's forms, the misfits, the sandbox's words, a project's values, the cap and the hundred values). Every break is caught by the test it names. One flow that is one story already, a project's extension through its trust (a yes, the next session, a change asked about, a yes again and a link out, a session behind the link, a name of the user's), is one test, its sessions one after another (`extensions_found::a_projects_extension_walks_through_its_trust`); the rest stay one rule a test.

**What remains is a conflict with the tier's budget (rule 11).** Both trees on one hosted Windows runner, medians of 5: before the fold, tier 2 was 38.3 s against main's 33.2, +5.1 s, at 6 ms a process start (run 44873812174); after the fold, on a slower runner (8 ms), 51.1 against 45.5, +5.6 s (44879370107); after the chain, 30.0 against 27.4, +2.6 s, at 6 ms (44881859246). The core's share went from +34% of main's to +25% (11.11 to 13.89 s), the extension crate's left the tier for tier 1, and the tier is at 75% of its 40 s: above the aim of main and about 2 s, and on a slow runner nearer the budget. What it costs is the sessions: on a hosted Windows runner, in a release build, a session's open took about 7 ms and its first turn 4 to 10 ms, about 20 ms with its world, core and close (a throwaway timing test, run there during the fold); in CI's build it is about twice that. Folding shares a world and a core between cases but keeps each case's session and turn, which is why the tests' count fell further than their cost. **Further folding was not done**, by the maintainers' choice: a test is one rule, so that a break's failure names its rule, and chaining cases that are not one story would let an early step's failure hide a later rule. **The lever** is the harness's own: a session's open and first turn on Windows (what they read and write before the first request), or a tier budget that counts the extensions' tests, which the maintainers are to settle with the CI budgets.
