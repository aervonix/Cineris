# Signed approvals, part: the password prompt

The place the approvals password is typed, at a terminal and in the application, built on step 25's key input. The design is this folder's `answered.md`, "The password prompt"; the description as built is [09-tui.md](../../../architecture/09-tui.md), "The approvals password", and [08-configuration.md](../../../architecture/08-configuration.md), "Credentials". Signing, the key file, the subcommands that ask, and opening the dialog at a session's start or a question's yes are other parts'.

## What was built

- **`cineris_base::Password`** (`secret_buf.rs`): a `SecretBuf` that is never trimmed and gives only its bytes, `expose_bytes()`, named as the place they can leak; `chars()`, `is_empty()`, `Password::LEAST` (12), `From<SecretBuf>`. `Debug` says only how many characters; not `Clone`, `Display` or `Serialize`; wiped when dropped. `SecretBuf` gained `expose_bytes()` and `same_as()` (a whole comparison with no copy), and the module `wipe_string(String)`, which writes zeros over a string's whole allocation before it is freed.
- **`cineris_tui::secret::password`**, re-exported from `secret`:
  - `PasswordInput`, the field: a sibling of `KeyInput` over the same buffer, 1 KiB. Typing (what types a character into the prompt types it here), a paste, `Backspace`, `Ctrl+U`, `Enter`, `Esc`. Not trimmed; any text but control characters; one line break at a paste's end dropped; a paste with one inside, a control character or too much refused whole with a sentence.
  - `PasswordAsk { purpose, lines, warning }` and `Purpose::{Unlock, Sign, Choose}`.
  - `PasswordPrompt`: the purpose's sentence, the caller's lines drawn whole, the warning, the field (`rows(width, height, shown, look) -> PromptRows { lines, field }`, `keys(field)`), and for `Choose` the second typing: under 12 characters, or a second typing that differs, says so and asks again. `key`, `repeat`, `paste` and `cancel` give `Prompted::{Changed, Nothing, Done(Option<Password>)}`. `Ctrl+C` and `Esc` answer with nothing and wipe the field and a first typing.
  - At a terminal: `read_password(overrides, &ask)`, `PasswordTerminal::{enter, read, ending, leave}` (taken before anything slow, as `KeyTerminal` is, and able to ask again with a warning), and `read_password_on(&mut Terminal<D>, &ask)` for tests.
- **In the application** (`app/state/password.rs`): `App::ask_password(ask, handler)`, whose handler, `FnOnce(&mut App, Option<Password>) + Send`, is called once the dialog has gone; `App::asking_password()`. While it is open every key and paste is its own; one asked while another is open waits behind it; the viewer is closed when it opens; what it hid starts its grace again when it goes.
- **Type-ahead.** Both hold the dialogs' rule (`app::ready::Readiness`): the field is drawn, and takes keys, no sooner than 300 ms after the first frame, a quiet second after the last input it did not take (dropped), and the repeat window after the key that answered what came before. At a terminal the input that has arrived is also read and thrown away once the first frame is written, and every event whose first byte was read by then. It is read rather than flushed with `tcflush` or `FlushConsoleInputBuffer`: the replies to the terminal's capability queries share the input, and a flush could cut one in two and leave its tail to be typed. `Ctrl+C` answers with nothing whenever it comes after the first frame, so that a way out is never held back by the quiet.
- **The terminal closed to other programs** (`cineris-term`): `Terminal::set_exclusive(on)` and `is_exclusive()`, through a new `Device::exclusive` (a no-op by default); on Unix `TIOCEXCL` and `TIOCNXCL`, turned off by the terminal's end, by the emergency restore (a panic, a signal) and by a stop, and on again when the program is continued; on Windows nothing. The prompt turns it on from before its first frame to after its rows are erased; the application's loop turns it on while `App::asking_password()`.
- **Paste strings wiped:** a paste's `String` is written over once taken or dropped, at the password's prompt, at the key's (`read_key_on`), and in the application's loop for every paste.
- **Changed outside the files first named,** at the maintainers' later request (the exclusive mode, the paste strings) and to share one rule: `crates/cineris-tui/src/app.rs` (`mod ready` made `pub(crate)` so the terminal's prompt holds the same rule; the loop's exclusive mode and its paste wipe), `crates/cineris-term/src/terminal.rs` and `sys/{unix,windows}.rs` (the exclusive mode).

- `cineris-tui`, at a terminal on a device of the tests' own (`at_a_terminal_…`): type-ahead (typing and its `Enter`, a paste, `Ctrl+C`, an `Esc` held for what might follow) dropped; a paste begun before the prompt dropped whole however late its end; keys before the field is shown dropped; no byte of any character of the password ever written to the terminal; the terminal closed to others from before the first frame until after, whichever way it ends; a paste taken, one with a line break inside refused; `Esc`, `Ctrl+C` and an early `Ctrl+C` give nothing and erase the rows; a password chosen by typing it twice, with the too-short and mismatch rounds; a window too small takes no key and `Ctrl+C` still goes back.

- Breaks: `mutations/signed-approvals-password-prompt.breaks`, eight, each naming the tests that catch it. Checked by the pre-flight only (`cargo xtask mutate --check`); not run here.

## What it does not stop, and what is left

- **The exclusive mode** keeps a program that was not reading the terminal from opening it. It does not stop: a process that already has the terminal open (opened before, or inherited: a job the shell started, a command run earlier that kept it), which reads on and can race the harness for each key; root; a process that can trace the harness (Yama's `ptrace_scope` 0) and take its descriptor; the terminal emulator and a multiplexer, which see every key; a keyboard read below the terminal (X11, a low-level hook on Windows). Windows has no such mode: another process attached to the same console can read its input. While it is on, a program of the harness's own that opens the terminal (a command asking for a password on `/dev/tty`) is refused too.
- **Copies outside the buffer:** the terminal layer's read buffer and, on Unix, the system read's on the stack; the decoder's buffer, in which a paste is gathered, which grows and is cleared rather than written over, its earlier allocations freed as they were; the copies a string leaves where it grew from; and a typed character on its way, a `char` on the stack. They are the process's memory, short-lived, and never written anywhere.
- **A way out that leaves the terminal broken:** `SIGKILL` (the out-of-memory killer's included) and a build with `panic = "abort"`, which no shipped profile is, end the process with no restore: the terminal is left without echo (`reset` or `stty sane` puts it back) and, if a password was being asked, in its exclusive mode on Unix, which stays until the terminal is closed. Nothing to fix there.
- **The controlling terminal:** the prompt reads standard input and writes standard output, as the key's does, where the design says `/dev/tty` and `CONIN$`. A subcommand run with standard input or output redirected cannot ask. To decide with the subcommands; the terminal layer would need to open the controlling terminal itself.
- **The full screen's search row:** with the full-screen presenter and its search open, the search's row is drawn over the dialog's hint row (`state/screen.rs` replaces the last row while no dialog of the queue is up). Keys still go to the dialog. A one-line change there, to leave the row while `asking_password()`, is the wiring's.
- **Unicode normalization:** the password's bytes are the text as typed; a composed and a decomposed `é` are two passwords. Normalizing would need a new dependency.
- **Untested on Windows** until the runners: all of it, though nothing here is Windows' own but the no-op exclusive mode. The exclusive mode is untested against a real terminal until the subcommands' pty tests.
- **Records:** this folder's `answered.md` must link `parts/` once for `cargo xtask rules` to pass.
