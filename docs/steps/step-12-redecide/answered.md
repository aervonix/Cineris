## The design, before building

Written before the code, read by the reviewer, and sent to the coordinator. What the build departed from is said under "As built", below.

**The rule.** When a call's turn to start comes, it is **planned again and decided again** if, since it was decided, either a call that may have changed the file system has finished, or the policy's rules have narrowed. The loop runs the tool's `plan` again, shows the new plan to `Policy::decide`, and acts on the new decision as it acted on the first: allow, and the call is scheduled by its new plan; ask, and the question is put; deny, and the call gets its denial; stop, and the turn stops at it, in the model's order. Until the second decision is in, the call is a call whose plan is not known yet: nothing after it that could conflict with it starts, and no question after it is put. Where nothing changed, nothing more is done.

**The two counts.**

- **The round counts the calls that finish** that may have changed the file system. A call's decision is dated by that count **when its planning starts**, not when the loop reads its report: a link made while the plan was being worked out is then after the decision, and the call is decided again.
- **The policy counts its narrowings** (`Policy::narrowed`, a number that only grows). The engine adds one when an approval is revoked (`Engine::revoke`) and when a learned command that was in force is taken off (`Engine::set_learned`); the core adds one when it puts another engine in force for a change of level. A call's decision is dated by this count too, read at the same moment. A change of level counts whichever way it goes: it is rare, and a second decision costs microseconds.

| Point | Settled as | Why |

**What counts as a call that may have changed the file system:** a call the round started (a tool run began) whose plan has any effect that is not a read, or that is not scheduled as read-only. That is a shell command (one the engine took as only reading included: the program may do otherwise, and the sandbox looks after those too), `write`, `edit`, and an extension's or an MCP server's tool, which is exclusive unless it declares otherwise and has its `ext` effect. Not counted: `read`, `search`, `list`, `outline`, and `plan` and `remember`, which change only the session; a call that was denied, refused when planned, or never started.

**Which calls are decided again: every call, not only those with an `exec` or `write` effect.** This goes beyond the agreed text, and was built as a recommendation that one predicate undoes; decided by the owner on 2026-11-16, as built. The twin, for reads: with `ln -s ~ d` as one call and `read d/.config/cortex-harness/credentials.toml` as a later call of the same response, the read is planned while `d` does not exist, judged as a file in the workspace, and allowed; `read` opens the planned path, the system follows `d` on the way, and the harness's own credentials, which the built-in protection closes to reading, are read into the context and sent to the provider. `ln -s ~ d` then `read d/.aws/credentials` reads beyond the scope the same way, and `search` of `d` starts its walk at a name that is now a link (whether the walker goes through a root that is a link is to be tested; its gate does not ask about the scope again). At `autonomous` the `ln` runs unasked (`cargo xtask decide --level autonomous -- 'ln -s ~ d'`: allow), and the sandbox does not limit reads. It costs nothing where nothing changed; where something did, a read that waited behind the command is planned again, a few system calls.

**What the sandbox's look at protected paths already catches,** for a confined command, and what it does not:

- **Catches:** a confined command that writes a protected path in the workspace, through a link or not (`ln -s . d` then `cp evil d/.bashrc`; `ln -s .git d` then `cp evil d/hooks/pre-commit`): found after the command, the turn stops, and what the path held is kept for undo. A write outside the write roots through a link (`ln -s ~ d` then `cp evil d/.bashrc`) is refused by Landlock.
- **Does not catch:** anything where the sandbox does not enforce (Windows, a kernel without Landlock 3, `sandbox.enabled = false`), at `unrestricted`, or for a command the user let leave the sandbox; `write` and `edit`, which are not commands (step 10's check at the moment of writing refuses them, with an error for the model and no question); a read through a link (above); a protected file the list does not name. And it detects, after the fact: the file was written, and the user has to look and undo. The second decision asks first, on every OS and at every level that asks.

**The cost when nothing changed,** against the row "Tool call arguments complete → read-only tool started" (≤ 1 ms): one read of the policy's count when a call's planning starts, and, when the round looks for what may start, one read of it and two comparisons for each call that may start. The engine's count is an atomic; the core's adds a read of the lock that holds the engine in force. Measured in the step's report. A second decision costs one `plan` (on the blocking pool, like the first) and one `decide`, as the first decision did.

The coordinator, for the owner, agreed the note on 2026-11-16: the four points and the two counts as set out, the folder's name, and deciding every call again, reads included, as a tightening passed to the owner. The owner decided it on 2026-11-16, as built.

## As built

| Point | Where the build departed from the note, and why |
|---|---|
| **When a call is decided again** | **At once, not at its turn.** The review of the design found that a call whose decision a change made stale, left waiting behind a running call, held nothing back: a later call with side effects could start, and the stale call's second decision could then be a question, or a stop, after it. So as soon as the round looks at what may start, every call that has not started and whose date is behind a count is planned and decided again, in the model's order; until it is in, it is a call whose plan is not known. One check at the top of `Round::pump`, in place of a check at each call's turn and before each question. |
| **The model's order** | **Two more rules.** While a call that may change the file system runs, and an answer may be a stop, a later call with side effects waits for every earlier call that has not started (it will be decided again when the running call ends). And a call planned again onto a path that a later call has already used does not run, and says so: with the first fix only a non-exclusive tool that makes links can bring it about. |
| **1. What a person allowed** | **Kept by the loop's own id for the call and the call's accesses**, not by the provider's id and the question alone. The review found both too loose: a provider's id repeats (a server that gives none has `call_1` made up in every response), and a command's reasons name its words, not where it runs, so a command allowed in `sub`, planned again after `sub` became a link, asked the same question about another place. `PlannedCall` gains `item`, the call's result item's id. The check is made in `decide` only: `ask` is reached for a call `decide` answered "ask", whose question was not the one kept. |
| **What counts as a change** | **A command the policy found only reads does not count.** Every part of such a command is on the table of commands that only read, none of which makes a link; counting it would decide later calls again for nothing, and a read-only command can run beside them, so the count was the wrong guard for it anyway (the review). A tool from an extension that declares itself read-only or keyed and makes a link, running beside a later call, is a stated limit: [outstanding/a-tool-that-makes-links-beside-other-calls.md](../../outstanding/a-tool-that-makes-links-beside-other-calls.md). |
| **3. The check at the moment of writing** | As the note said, with one correction from the review: step 10's `still_leads_there` resolves the path at the start of the change, and again just before the write only when the call made directories. What the note said of the gap stands. |
| **After the sandbox stops the turn** | **A behaviour change, kept** (the coordinator, 2026-11-16). The stopping command counts as a change, so every call of the turn that has not started is decided again, and the core's policy answers each with the stop: the turn stops at the first of them, a read included. Before, a read after the stop still ran, and only calls with side effects were refused (`StopsWithTurn`, which stays). It is what [architecture/06-permissions-and-sandbox.md](../../architecture/06-permissions-and-sandbox.md) already said: no later call of the turn is decided as anything but a stop. Step 26's tests pass unchanged. |
| **The working directory a call gives** (found while building, fixed with the coordinator's agreement) | **A command that may write is judged where the call's own `cwd` puts it,** as it was after a `cd`. `cargo xtask decide --level autonomous` before and after, with `make`: `--cwd /work/app/.git/hooks` and `--cwd /work/app/.git` ALLOW, now ASK ("The call would write `/work/app/.git/hooks`, which is a protected path…"); `--cwd /work/app/.cortex` ALLOW, now ASK; `--cwd /tmp/elsewhere` (outside the workspace) ASK before and after, through the write of it the tool declares; `--cwd /work/app/src` and the root ALLOW before and after; `ls` in `.git/hooks` read-only before and after. A planned-again call's `cwd` is resolved through a link, so without this a link to `.git` would have moved a command there unasked at `autonomous`. |
| **A revoke during a change of level** (found by the review, fixed with the coordinator's agreement) | **The new engine takes over the approvals in force when it is put in force**, and a revoke takes the engine in force under the same lock (`EngineInForce` in `cortex-core`'s `policy`; `Engine::keep_approvals_of`; the full review below added the recording of an answer). Before, a level change made its engine with the old one's approvals, wrote the lease, and only then put it in force: a revoke in between reached the old engine alone, and its count of narrowings with it. Two lines change in `core.rs`. |
