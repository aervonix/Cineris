## A terminal's reply typed into the input box

**What happened.** At its start the program asks the terminal for its keyboard protocol and its device attributes, the last reply. Through a pseudo-console the replies come about a key at a time, and on a busy Windows machine the rest of a reply came more than the 500 ms a lone ESC was held for: the ESC was taken as the Escape key and `[?0u` was typed into the prompt, where a person could send it. It failed `tui::sys_a_session_is_resumed_with_its_history_drawn` and `tui::sys_a_turn_with_tools_runs_in_the_interface_and_the_terminal_is_put_back`, and it was also the cause of `tui::sys_a_turn_is_interrupted_mid_stream_and_mid_tool`'s timeout: that job's screen shows `> [?0u` in the box, so the first frame's placeholder never came.

**The rule now** ([conpty.md](../../conpty.md), "Wait for the rest of a reply"; [09-tui.md](../../architecture/09-tui.md)). While the replies are due (until the device attributes come, and only within 3 s of the queries):

- a lone ESC is held until the device attributes come, or 3 s after the queries, and at least 500 ms after it came;
- after 50 ms it is released at once before what can begin no sequence (a character, a control byte); within 50 ms `ESC x` is still Alt+x;
- what can begin a sequence is read with the ESC, so a focus report, a key's sequence or a paste mark split after its ESC is still what it is; an ESC that waited alone and whose sequence never came is the Escape key, then the rest;
- a reply that lost its ESC (`[?…u`, `[?…c`, `[?…$y`, `]11;…`) is read as the reply and never typed, and a `[` or `]` that may begin one is held as an ESC is.

**What it costs a person.** An Escape, `[` or `]` pressed while the replies are due comes through when the device attributes come, at once if a key that begins no sequence follows it, and at the latest 3 s after the queries; on a terminal that is not loaded the replies come within milliseconds, so nothing is waited for. Keys after a held one come through in order, and nothing is lost. A person who types `Esc [ ? 0 u` in those 3 s has it taken as the reply. The test `terminal::tests::a_reply_whose_escape_comes_a_second_before_its_rest_types_nothing` drives a reply whose ESC comes 1 s before its rest, on the fake clock, and fails with the fix taken out.

**A held key is judged by when it was pressed.** A dialog can be up while the replies are due (the trust dialog comes just after the first frame), and a key the decoder held was stamped with the time it was read, so an Escape pressed before a dialog was ready could answer it, up to 3 s later. The maintainers settled it, with the review's agreement: the terminal says when each event's first byte came (`Terminal::came`), and the application uses that time only to judge whether a key came before a dialog, a panel or a first-run step was ready or within its grace; for the quiet second that any input starts again, and for the time a key was taken, a held key counts when it was read, the later time, so no quiet second is cut short. Only keys and pastes behind an Escape now make it part of a burst of typing. Tests: `dialogs::a_held_escape_pressed_before_the_dialog_was_ready_does_not_answer_it`, `dialogs::a_key_just_after_a_held_escape_is_read_does_not_answer`, `dialogs::a_paste_split_across_reads_holds_the_dialog_from_its_end`, `dialogs::keys_read_when_they_are_pressed_are_taken_as_before`, `dialogs::a_held_escape_that_answers_holds_the_next_dialog_from_when_it_was_read`, and through the loop `app::tests::an_escape_the_terminal_held_does_not_answer_a_dialog_it_was_pressed_before`.

**Two cases found by the review of step 25, covered by the hold:** a split reply at the first run no longer goes into the provider key's input (`first_run::a_reply_split_after_its_escape_never_goes_into_the_key`), and a split reply no longer cancels `cineris auth login`, which sends the queries on its own terminal (`secret::tests::auth_login_is_neither_cancelled_nor_typed_into_by_a_split_reply`).

**Left in [input-decoder-corners](../../outstanding/input-decoder-corners.md):** a person's key typed inside a reply; replies that may come as console input records once that input mode is on (inferred, not measured); and a mouse report split after its ESC after the start-up window, which is the Escape key and text as before this fix (the mouse is on only in the alternate screen, and never on Windows).

The four breaks of the hold in [`ci-budgets.breaks`](../../../mutations/ci-budgets.breaks) are aimed at the code as it is now, and one's rule, that the hold is never longer than 500 ms, is rewritten.

## Three flakes made deterministic

- **The base capture** (`trust::sys_a_project_trusted_in_the_store_is_trusted_while_its_rules_are_the_ones_the_user_saw`, `harness_git::sys_the_base_state_is_taken_by_a_git_inside_the_sandbox`, `gates::sys_the_base_state_keeps_only_what_the_agent_could_read_unasked`). The capture could come out incomplete beside the turn's own git, or not be recorded when it outlasted the close's 2 s wait. An awaited point on the capture's thread after it hands its state over (`cineris_session::BASE_STATE_HANDED_OVER`, nothing without the test hooks) lets the core's rig wait for the capture: the trust tests before their turn, the other two before the close. The capture and the close's wait are unchanged. With the capture held past the close on purpose, the test without the wait recorded nothing and with it recorded the base. Under `stress --strict --count 200 --cores 2 --load 6` the three together passed 599 runs of 600, the base recorded and complete in every one; the one failure was the shell's look giving up as too slow during a disk stall (46%), the mechanism of [a-git-look-too-slow-on-a-loaded-windows-runner](../../outstanding/a-git-look-too-slow-on-a-loaded-windows-runner.md), whose known flakes now include the trust test. The trust tests take longer, as the capture no longer runs beside their turn (about 2.3 s to 3 s for the longest, on the reference machine).
- **The put-back test** (`harness_git::sys_a_hooks_folder_made_a_link_to_a_planted_hook_is_taken_away`). `tools/pre-commit`, written where nothing watched, is first watched when the link makes it a hook, and whether the look names it depended on its change time against the look's start. The test now holds the second turn's command after its look before (`BEFORE_THE_COMMAND_STARTS`) and moves the file's change time there, so it is always named, and holds the property itself: the link gone, the planted hook not reached, the turn stopped, the model not asked again, the file's content kept. With the old assertion it fails as CI saw it. 200 of 200 under `stress --strict`. No other test leans on that timing in its assertions.
- **The interrupted turn**: the reply typed into the prompt, above.
