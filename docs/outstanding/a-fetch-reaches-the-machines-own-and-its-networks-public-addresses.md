An extension's fetch does not refuse the machine's own addresses, or its network's, when they are addresses of the internet: a name a wildcard in `net` allows can be pointed at one.

| **What reaches them.** `cineris_base::address::classify` refuses ranges by prefix, and on a dual-stack network the machine's own IPv6 addresses and every host's on its network are global (`2000::/3`), as are an intranet's on public addresses (a university's, many enterprises' on IPv6) and a machine's with a public IPv4 address on an interface. A script whose manifest has `net = ["*.attacker.example"]` learns the machine's address from its own server, points a name it controls at it (or at the router, `<prefix>::1`), and fetches it; with `*`, a name inside the intranet reaches the intranet. Connecting to one of the machine's own addresses reaches every service bound to `::` or `0.0.0.0`. Step 28's second review found it. **Its reach:** a name goes only over `https`, and the certificate must be for the URL's host, so most local services receive a connection and a TLS greeting and no request; a request reaches only a service whose certificate matches a host the manifest allows. The error says only that the fetch could not connect, whatever the cause, but how long a failure takes still tells a refused connection from one that answered and failed its TLS, so a script can still map the open ports, more slowly. **The bound is the manifest:** `net = ["*"]` and wide patterns are what make these addresses reachable, so the words a wildcard entry is approved with (the trust question of step 28's second pull request, `cineris ext add` in its third) should say what it reaches: "any host on the internet, including addresses on your own network that have public addresses". **The words are in** since step 28's part 2a-1: the trust question shows a `*` in `net` as "any host on the internet, including addresses on your own network that have public addresses" (`cineris ext add`, in the third part, takes the same text). **Recommendation:** in step 32, refuse the machine's own interface addresses and the networks they are on (`getifaddrs` on Linux, `GetAdaptersAddresses` on Windows, behind a seam that a test answers, read for each fetch or when the interfaces change), as a kind of their own.
