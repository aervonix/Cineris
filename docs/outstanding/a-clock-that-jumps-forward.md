| **A clock that jumps forward prunes and folds the statistics early.** The writer prunes the monthly files by its own clock's month, and folds day buckets by its clock's day. A clock set months ahead deletes the raw files early: their totals stay in the aggregates, but the per-request context records the context profiler (step 44) reads are lost. One set more than 400 days ahead folds every day bucket into its month for good, so today, week and month, and a day's limits, read nothing for those days once the clock is right again. Found by the independent review of step 16 as a note, and confirmed by reading: the files themselves hold no date the writer could check the clock against, since a jumped clock stamps the events it writes with the jumped date, and a jump forward cannot be told from a machine that was off for months. To settle: a guard that waits before an irreversible step (a prune of more than one month at once, a fold of buckets the aggregates gained in the last few days), or a high-water mark of the latest day the writer has seen kept in the aggregates, against which a later clock earlier than it is caught. Real-time clocks that reset usually reset to the past, which is harmless here. | Step 33, or the step that next changes the statistics' retention | Before the context profiler (step 44) relies on the raw files |
