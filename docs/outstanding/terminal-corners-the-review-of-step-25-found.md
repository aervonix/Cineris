| **Found by the review of step 25, at lower confidence, by reading.** (1) **`CSI M` read where no report was asked for.** The decoder reads `CSI M` and three bytes as a mode-1000 mouse report whenever the application has the mouse on (`self.mouse`), not only where the terminal was asked for reports (`Terminal::asks_vt_mouse`): on Windows, and wherever the win32 input mode is on, nothing asks for them ([the-mouse-under-windows-terminal](the-mouse-under-windows-terminal.md)), and what follows such a `CSI M` is taken for a report and dropped. (2) **The paste-burst thresholds were never measured:** where pastes are not known to come bracketed, 8 keys within 50 ms are taken for a paste (`BURST`, `crates/cineris-term/src/terminal.rs`), numbers no measurement on a real terminal or link set; a paste whose last segment comes late has its `Enter` read as a key, which sends. (3) **The start could turn off mouse modes a crashed program left on,** as `LEFT_ON` does after the external editor (`crates/cineris-tui/src/external.rs`), so that the viewer's or the full screen's mouse is the only one asked for. (4) **A stalled SSH link can block the Unix emergency handler** (`crates/cineris-term/src/sys/unix.rs`, lines 179-197 and 267-276 at the review's commit, `77ffbbcc`): the handler writes the restore and calls `tcsetattr(TCSADRAIN)` before it arms its 3 s alarm, with the other signals masked, so a write that never drains holds it, and the process with it. Older than step 25. **Recommendation:** (1) read `CSI M` as a report only where reports were asked of the terminal; (2) measure the thresholds on a real terminal over a slow link, and decide what a late last segment's `Enter` does; (3) turn the mouse modes off in `START`; (4) arm the alarm before the write (step 32). | Step 33 (terminal) for (1) to (3); step 32 (hardening) for (4) | Before the first release |
