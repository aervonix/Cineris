| **A year's usage report on Windows is over one frame.** [performance-budgets.md](../performance-budgets.md) holds the usage view with a year of history to ≤ 16 ms from the aggregates. Step 24's third part measured a generated year (365 days, 62,835 requests, five providers, eleven models, twenty projects, two speeds; `cineris-stats`' `perf_a_years_report_for_the_usage_panel`, release build, 320 reports, medians and 99th percentiles). **Reading the aggregates every report:** 17.4 / 19.4 ms on the desktop, 40.1 / 81.2 ms on a hosted Windows runner (try run 43995127341; 33.9 / 39.9 ms on another, run 43978349712). **With the aggregates kept** (`cineris-core`'s `usage::AggregatesCache`, step 24): 7.2 / 8.2 ms on the desktop, **16.1 / 27.9 ms on the runner**. What is left on the runner is bringing the kept aggregates up to date with the monthly files (8.3 ms at the median) and making the report from a year's days (8.9 ms). The panel itself opens in the key's frame, its history `reading…`, and draws the rows from the answer in under 1.5 ms; the rows come two frames later on that runner. The budget is the owner's to keep or change; the coordinator kept it. **Recommendation, in order:** (1) bring the aggregates up to date only from the files' ends past what was read (a file's length and time before it is opened), so that a report with nothing new reads no monthly file; (2) keep a year's days in a form the report can sum without walking every day's map (per-month totals beside the days, the days read only for today, the week and the month); (3) measure again on a hosted Windows runner, and only then decide whether the budget, the runner or the code gives. | The owner; then a step that touches `cineris-stats` | Before the first release, if the budget stands |
