| **The test of a terminal closing under a job of the application fails now and then, under heavy load and on a hosted runner.** `cortex-tui`'s `signals::sys_closing_the_terminal_ends_as_sighup` and `signals::sys_closing_the_terminal_of_a_leader_ends_as_sighup` (the application has its terminal's other end closed under it, as a job of a leader that ends, or as the leader itself) each wrote the first frame's mark and nothing more. The leader's case passed 99 of 100 runs of `cargo xtask stress -p cortex-tui -p cortex-term --count 100 --cores 2 --load 6 -E 'test(/signals::|handoff::/)'` at the desk after the fixes of step 23's review, and in the one failure the child died within 5 s. The job's case then failed once on a hosted Linux runner with no load added (pull request 74's `test-linux`, run 43879303889, job 133340593532), its report holding only `drawn` after the test's 30 s. A blocking test that fails on a hosted runner fails pull requests that never touched it, and a child that neither ends nor reports for 30 s after its terminal closes may be the program outliving its terminal, a defect of the product, not only of the test. Two causes are possible, neither confirmed: the 3 s watchdog of the hand-over (`alarm(3)`) firing in a child whose end took longer, or the hand-over itself not reaching its end. Recommendation: have the test's child note the watchdog's firing (a SIGALRM handler of the test's own that writes to its report) and where it waits, run the two tests many times on a hosted runner (`try`, `stress -p cortex-tui --count 200` with a filter on them), find which side stops, and fix the cause with a test, or make the test deterministic if only the test is wrong. | A branch of its own after step 23's review fixes merge (`worktree-step-23-fixes-hangup`), as a third pull request of those fixes | Before the first release |
