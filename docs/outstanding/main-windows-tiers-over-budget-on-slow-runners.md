On a slow Windows runner, `main`'s own integration and system tiers are over their budgets, so a pull request that lands on one shows warnings it did not cause.

| **What was measured.** Step 28's second pull request ran both trees on one Windows runner at 8 ms a process start, five rounds each (`try` 44839011960). `main`'s side, medians of five: tier 1 22.5 s of 25 (90%), **tier 2 46.7 s of 40 (117%)**, **tier 3 90.1 s of 90 (100%)**; the pull request moved each by at most 0.4 s. The step's first pull request, on a runner at 5 ms (`try` 44819307542), had `main` at 10.7, 22.5 and 54.9 s. Hosted Windows runners differ in speed by a factor of two ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), "Windows facts"): CI warns above a budget and fails at one and a half times it, so on a slow runner tier 2 is 13 s from failing and tier 3 45 s, and every pull request there reads as over budget. **The levers** are those the CI-budgets fix found and used ([steps/ci-budgets/answered.md](../steps/ci-budgets/answered.md), "Where the time went" and "What was changed"): the tests' own growth, read tier by tier; on Windows each of `cineris-core`'s integration tests costing 28 ms above its process's start (a core started, 6 ms, and a session opened, 23 ms), where the 23 ms go not yet known; the git-heavy tests run on Windows in the nightly; cheaper commands and smaller bounds in tests; and running a tier's tests two at a time, which Linux's tiers 1 and 2 do. **What step 28's part 2a-1 adds, and the lever.** Its extension tests added about 5 s to Windows' tier 2 at first; folding them, moving what needs no core into unit tests and chaining the flow that is one story brought that to +2.6 s on one runner (`try` 44881859246: 30.0 s against 27.4, at a 6 ms start; [its record](../steps/28/parts/pr2a1-found.md)). What is left is the cost of a session, not of a test: on Windows, in a release build, a session's open is about 7 ms and its first turn 4 to 10 ms, about 20 ms with the world, the core and the close, and about twice that in CI's build. So **the lever for every core test is a session's open and first turn on Windows** (the 23 ms a session's open was measured at before, above); a test is one rule, so a break's failure names its rule, and the tests are not folded further. Later parts of step 28 build their core tests on in-memory sessions and shared worlds, chain the flows that are one story, and look at `cargo xtask ci --tiers --like-runner` before each pull request. On a slow runner the margin of tier 2 to its failure at 60 s is now about 9 s. **The job's own time limit is now within reach too:** `main`'s Windows test job at `36d8266e` took 255 s on a runner at 6 ms a process start (run 44886379853: the build 1 min 37 s, tiers 1 and 2 46.7 s, the system tests 42.0 s), and on a runner about 1.3 times slower, as step 28's fourth pull request met (run 44911679902: the build 2 min 11 s, tiers 1 and 2 60.8 s, then cut at 5 min 5 s in the system tests), it would take about 330 s, past the five minutes the blocking job is given.
